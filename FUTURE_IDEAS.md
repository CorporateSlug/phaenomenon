## Community queer events noticeboard

**Status:** Parked / explore later  
**Origin:** Aaron's idea, discussed 4 November 2026  
**Product boundary:** Potential standalone, community-facing project; not part of the current private Phaenomenon production management workflows.

### The idea

A very lightweight local queer-community event listing website. People and community organisers could submit their own events with **minimal effort**, without needing to run advertising, build elaborate promotional material or learn a full event-management platform.

Think of it as a simple, useful community calendar / noticeboard rather than a social network, ticketing service, promotional agency or complex event planner.

**Possible minimum viable experience:**
- A publicly browsable, date-ordered list/calendar of local events.
- A short self-service submission form: event name, approximate or confirmed date/time, location (if public), short description and optional external ticket/RSVP/info link.
- A clear distinction between tentative/community-submitted information and confirmed event details.
- Basic categories/tags and straightforward ways to update/cancel entries.
- Light moderation and abuse/spam reporting, without a complicated organiser dashboard.
- Mobile-first, low-friction and intentionally minimalist; no requirement to design posters, upload media, manage campaigns or sell tickets on-site.

### Relationship with Phaenomenon

Phaenomenon's **internal event record** handles a production's people, venues, stages, technical design, staffing, costs and closeout. This idea concerns **public community discovery and submissions**. Do not assume every community-listed event should become a Phaenomenon job or appear in its internal bookings.

If developed separately in future, an **explicit opt-in publishing/linking workflow** could let Aaron list a Phaenomenon-led public event on the noticeboard, or import a publicly submitted event as an internal opportunity. This should never automatically expose unpublished production details, crew identities, contact information or private locations.

### Questions to revisit later

- Is the community board its own brand/site, or deliberately associated with Phaenomenon?
- Is the geographic scope Melbourne only, or broader?
- Who can post: public submissions with moderation, approved organisers, or a combination?
- What is the minimal moderation/verification approach, especially for sensitive private community events and venues?
- How long should events remain listed, and who can edit or remove them?
- Would linking to existing ticketing/RSVP pages be enough (likely yes initially)?

### Explicitly out of scope for the first version

Native ticket sales, payments, heavy promotion, algorithms and feeds, social profiles, follower networks, messaging, complex organiser accounts, 3D production planning, analytics and automated marketing.

**When to revisit:** Only after the core Phaenomenon event lifecycle, design and delivery workflows are stable, or if Aaron independently decides the noticeboard is worth making as a small standalone experiment.
